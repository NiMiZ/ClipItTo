## ClipItTo 0.17.0: pictures and text from your phone, a self-test, and a panic button

**New (3)**
- **Receive from phone.** Settings › General › *Receive from phone…* (or `clipitto --phone`) opens a window with a QR code. Scan it with your phone's
  camera: a page opens in the phone's browser with *Take a picture*, *Choose from the gallery* (several at once) and a box for typing text.
  The picture goes straight to your PC over your own Wi-Fi: ClipItTo keeps it in the history and reads its **text**, which is added as a text
  copy (*Copy text* and *Paste* in the window). Typed text arrives the same way. Works with iPhone and Android (photos are converted to JPEG
  on the phone). The address has a secret and stops working after 5 minutes (*Stop* and *Extend* in the window); only devices on your local
  network can connect, and nothing goes through any server or the internet. It is plain HTTP, so anyone on the same Wi-Fi who sees the address
  could send until it expires. The first time, Windows or macOS asks whether to allow incoming connections: allow it for private networks.
  ClipItTo only listens while that window is open.
- **Check this installation.** Settings › General › *Check this installation…* (or `clipitto --selftest`) tests what works: the capture
  backend, a real test copy, the global shortcut, paste, the history database, the settings file, the keyrings, the password chain, the GNOME
  extension, the screen-lock watcher, text recognition, translation packs, sync and more. Each line is PASS, WARN, FAIL or SKIPPED with a reason and
  what to do, and *Copy report* gives text to send when something does not work. The report never contains clipboard contents.
- **Wipe everything now.** Settings › Passwords has a button that does what the panic key does (wipes the history, locks the passwords, empties the
  clipboard), after one question. The panic shortcut and `clipitto --panic` still do not ask.

**Also**
- ClipItTo now links Qt Network all the time (for the phone window); the `.deb` depends on it, the snap may bind a network port, and the macOS
  app explains the Local Network permission.
